# Draft stress tests: bidding priorities are not standings outcomes

## October 17, 2026 — unavailable acquisitions belong in the range

**Conversation evidence:** the user authorizes the league-specific prototype and
confirms active position slots. The agreed output must help compare baseline
contribution with plausible acquisition costs without overstating manager history.

**Assistant synthesis:** dropping unaffordable or position-incompatible cases can
make a difficult target look cheap. A buyer's already-full roster is a different
state from an open opportunity that cannot be completed. A final sale price can
also be far below what another buyer would have needed to outbid the winner.

**Practice:** keep open/closed/never-offered denominators separate, retain failed
open acquisitions as infinite costs for quantiles, and render the resulting
unavailable percentile explicitly. Match all roster slots, preserve minimum
reserves and test whole packages under shared conditions. Keep an empty bargain
screen as a valid result; do not loosen the rule after inspecting evaluation data.

**Evidence and follow-up:** the [research contract](../league-draft-research-contract.md)
and synthetic checks cover strict outbidding, censored ranges, slot reassignment,
identity/evidence gates and frozen discovery. Individual price calibration and
real losing bids remain unavailable. The delivered report is exploratory and
does not change personal ceilings or imply future trade/waiver profits.

## October 17, 2026 — contribution, competition and acquisition cost

**Conversation evidence:** after exploring the workshop, the user favors a balanced
build and asks for baseline and league-adjusted values using manager history.
They select a private prototype and request the analytical framework before code.

**Assistant synthesis:** opponent tendencies can change acquisition costs without
changing a player's projected contributions. Final category ranks cannot identify
draft intent, and a winning auction price does not reveal how much more the winner
would have paid. Missing losing bids and historical preseason expectations limit
what can be learned even when the archive contains many priced selections.

**Practice:** keep contribution baselines, league purchase estimates and personal
ceilings separate. Audit evidence dates, attribute draft decisions carefully,
validate predictions on later complete seasons and report assumptions where
preferences cannot be identified. Test complete packages rather than summing
individual discounts. No assumed future trade or waiver profit enters valuation.

**Follow-up:** [the framework](../league-adjusted-draft-value-framework.md) defines
the next implementation window and exploratory fallback. No model has been fitted
or new candidate list produced in this planning turn.

## October 17, 2026 — explain the decision before the metric

**Conversation evidence:** the user finds the initial report difficult to read
despite a strong quantitative background. They request plain explanations for
every strategy name, real player examples, actionable conclusions and interactive
exploration before choosing targets for the auction budget.

**Assistant synthesis:** technical accuracy is not enough when readers cannot
connect a metric to a manager decision. A short strategy label can hide two
different choices: accepted category weaknesses and spending concentration.
Presenting many score tables before examples makes that ambiguity harder to see.

**Practice:** separate those choices, explain what each buys and gives up, then
show a concrete player comparison and the next manager action. Keep detailed
methods accessible behind an optional appendix. Let the user explore difficult
examples and record preferences before preparing a target list. Use actual data
only in private reports; review the new flow with a synthetic mock first.

**Follow-up:** the user explicitly approves the synthetic workshop for the
real-data rewrite. The revised private guide separates the two choices, matches
saved auctions, holds purchases fixed when changing availability, and ends with
an exportable discussion brief. No numerical study or saved draft decisions change.

## October 17, 2026 — an analysis-focused draft request

**Conversation evidence:** the user asks how category scarcity, possible punts
and auction investment patterns can guide preparation, authorizing assumptions
and a Markdown/HTML analysis. The project already has a full saved projection
pool and a versioned dollar-allocation heuristic.

**Assistant synthesis:** model-dollar conservation does not prove a realizable
room allocation. A complete room needs unique purchases, per-team reserves and
actual budget debits. A zero bidding weight does not force last place in that
category: player correlations can retain it while sacrificing a different one.
Evaluate every category and show realized profiles rather than merely naming punts.

**Practice:** use a separate versioned research contract; freeze input features,
declare opponent and nomination rules, and share exogenous draws across policies.
Choose highlighted policies on training conditions and report all held-out core
results, with stress families separate. Use exact makes/attempts for ratios and
overflow-safe comparisons. Frequencies within an assumed room are not empirical
championship probabilities.

**Follow-up lesson:** a representative full-roster example can expose missing
position coverage. Keep that example and its failure visible; selecting a prettier
roster would hide model risk. Budget feasibility and nonempty position labels
do not establish a legal lineup. Static-versus-adaptive reversals also survive
passing tests and belong in the conclusion.

**Remaining evidence:** actual lineup rules, eligible pool, market behavior,
projection-error calibration and in-season management remain outside the study.
The full method is [the decision contract](../draft-robustness-study-contract.md).
Private player rows, numerical strategy outcomes and real roster examples stay
in ignored local reports.
